# THE FRONTIER

*A third-person low-fantasy survival exploration sandbox*

---

## What Is It?

You are a surveyor sent across the sea to chart a landmass no human has ever settled. The reason no one has settled it: it's full of monsters. An extinct civilization left behind ruins and strange places of power. The Old World wants the continent's riches -- furs, timber, iron, gold -- and your job is to go in first, survive, and decide where civilization takes root.

You explore. You hire a surveyor, camp at a promising site, and mark it for settlement. That site grows into a trading post, then a village, a town, and if you placed it well enough, a city. Roads form between your settlements. The areas around them become safe. The wilderness between them does not. And to the west, the monsters get worse.

The game does not give you a map. You start with a blank sheet and fill it in yourself -- surveying from hilltops with a spyglass, scanning the horizon for landmarks. You never see your position on the map. You learn the land by walking it.

## The Core Loop

1. **Prepare** at a settlement. Buy rations, repair gear, hire a porter.
2. **Explore** unknown territory. Navigate by compass and landmarks. Chop wood for campfires. Hunt rabbits for dinner.
3. **Discover** resources, ruins, and places of power. Scan everything into your journal.
4. **Found** a settlement by camping at a site with your Surveyor, waiting while he marks it out, and reporting back.
5. **Connect** your settlements with roads. Trade flows. Posts grow into villages, towns, cities.
6. **Push deeper** west. Behind you, the world you built is safe and thriving.

## What Makes It Different?

**You are on the ground, not above it.** This is not a city builder. You are the person standing at the edge of the unknown with a compass, a sword, and a pack full of rations. Every settlement exists because you walked there first.

**Infrastructure is progression.** No experience points. Your power comes from your trade network (better gear), your knowledge of the land (navigation), and permanent abilities gained from ancient places of power. Choose 3 of 4 disciplines at launch -- your build defines your playstyle permanently.

**The world transforms behind you.** Early areas that were terrifying become safe as settlements grow. A trading post barely keeps the monsters out. A city pacifies the surrounding area entirely.

**Settlements compete for trade.** Each settlement consumes resources flowing through it. A City eats 50% of everything passing through. Settlements at chokepoints -- river crossings, mountain passes -- naturally become economic centers. Where you found and in what order shapes your entire network.

**Preparation is gameplay.** Do you bring an axe for campfires, or a fishing rod for food? Rations are the best fuel but cost money. Hunting costs daylight. Running out of supplies deep in the west is dangerous, not fatal -- but the first monster you meet will be.

## The Setting

Low fantasy. Humans only. No magic in the Old World. The continent has an extinct precursor civilization whose ruins still function. The Old World is 17th-18th century: sailing ships, black powder firearms, mechanical clocks.

## Combat

Third-person, stamina-based. Melee weapons (blades, blunt), bows (silent, patient), and black powder firearms (loud, attract monsters). Monsters telegraph their attacks. Dodge-roll out of danger and counterattack. You see your character and the monster simultaneously -- timing is readable and fair.

Weapons and armor have rune slots. Runes are found in ruins, caches, and monster drops. Each rune's power is randomly rolled. Exploration is how you get better gear, and you can see it on your character -- glowing runes, enchanted weapons, upgraded armor.

## Disciplines

Ancient places of power grant permanent abilities. Four at launch, five more post-launch. You pick three. The choice is permanent.

| Discipline | Identity |
|---|---|
| **Survivalist** | Bow mastery, field dressing, dark vision, stealth, weatherproofing. The wilderness is your home. |
| **Warrior** | Heavy armor, carry weight, melee damage, stagger. The straightforward fighter. |
| **Artificer** | Mobile blacksmith, gun specialist. Repair gear, craft ammo, lay traps. Your camp is a workshop. |
| **Alchemist** | See hidden plants, lighter potions, poison resistance, advanced brewing. A walking pharmacy. |

Post-launch adds Ritualist, Swordsman, Wizard, Pathfinder, and Scholar -- bringing total builds to 84.

## The Economy

Raw materials flow east to port. Manufactured goods flow west to your settlements. Each settlement consumes a portion of trade passing through -- a City eats 50%. Cities are rare by design (exponential tier thresholds). On a mature map you might have 2-4 total. Each one is an achievement.

## Hirelings

Hire NPCs at settlements. They stay at your camp, bring their own tents, and never fight. Three types at launch:

| Type | Role |
|---|---|
| Porter | Extra carry capacity via backpack at camp. |
| Hunter | Auto-generates food at camp. No more hunting. |
| Surveyor | Assesses resources. Required for founding settlements. |

Post-launch adds Guide, Cartographer, and Healer.

## Platform and Scope

**Platform:** PC (Windows)
**Engine:** Godot 4, GDScript, Terrain3D plugin
**Camera:** Third-person
**Players:** Single player (co-op 2-4 post-launch)
**Target:** Solo indie development, AI-assisted, with contracted art assets
**Map:** 200-300 hex areas from modular templates. AI-assisted hex generation.

The game ships lean. Four disciplines. Three hirelings. Day/night but no seasons. No shields. Core loop proven, then expand.

*The Frontier occupies an identified gap: a third-person exploration game where your decisions about where to build civilization on a monster-haunted continent drive an economic system that visibly transforms the world.*
